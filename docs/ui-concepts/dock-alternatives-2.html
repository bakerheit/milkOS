1 Rooms: drawn 1:1 at 1024×768 and scaled to fit.

1. Rooms

Each Space is a tile that holds its own apps

medium

Familiar from

Spaces or virtual desktops, but shown as something you can read instead of a number.

What you see

The bar is not a list of apps. It is a list of places: Work has Files, Terminal and Notes, Play has Music and Paint. The lit room is the one you are in. Click another to go there, drop a window on a tile to send it, or press Cmd-Option and a number.

Detail

People who keep different jobs in different Spaces and lose track of which is which. It answers "where did I leave that?".

Build note

A strip over data the compositor already has: the window list per Space. The new work is drawing the app icons for each Space and the drop target.

Main elements

  • Room tiles
  • Name and window count
  • Small icons of what is open
  • Lit current room
  • + new room
  • Drop a window to move it

Font note

Names are short on purpose; counts use plain digits.

2 Recents strip: drawn 1:1 at 1024×768 and scaled to fit.

2. Recents strip

The dock shows what you touched, not what you own

medium

Familiar from

Jump lists and Recent Items, made into the main surface.

What you see

Four pinned apps sit at the left. Everything else is a row of cards, newest first: folders, files and terminals, each with a little picture of the window and how long ago you used it. The line under the cards has a tick wherever milkfs took a snapshot, so you can see how far back it goes.

Detail

Anyone who thinks in files and projects, not in programs. "Open the report I had open an hour ago" is one click.

Build note

Needs a short per-user history of opened paths and a small scaled picture saved with each one. The snapshot ticks come from the same list the Disk Utility shows. Shown by a key or by pushing at the bottom edge, so it costs nothing when hidden.

Main elements

  • Pinned apps
  • Cards, newest first
  • Window picture
  • Time label
  • Snapshot ticks
  • Fades after the fourth

Font note

Names cut at about 11 characters in Unifont.

3 Edge slots: drawn 1:1 at 1024×768 and scaled to fit.

3. Edge slots

Eight fixed spots on the border of the screen

easy

Familiar from

Hot corners, but each corner holds one app instead of one action.

What you see

Four corners and four edge middles, each with a small tab. Push the pointer into the corner and the tab grows to show the app and its name. Click, or just keep pushing, to go there. A corner is the one place you cannot miss, however fast you move.

Detail

Mouse users who want a big target and a tiny interface. On a 4:3 screen the corners are the cheapest real estate there is.

Build note

Hit-testing on the screen border, plus a grown tab drawn on top. Eight is a hard limit, which is the point: you pick your eight.

Main elements

  • Corner tabs
  • Edge-middle tabs
  • Grown tab with name
  • Running badge
  • Settings: choose the eight

Font note

Only the grown tab has text.

4 Keypad: drawn 1:1 at 1024×768 and scaled to fit.

4. Keypad

A fixed 3 by 3 grid that never reorders

easy

Familiar from

A phone keypad, or the number row you already use for Cmd-1 to Cmd-9.

What you see

Nine slots in a small square. Slot 5 is always Paint, slot 2 is always Terminal, so your hand learns the place. A number sits on each icon and is also the shortcut. Nothing slides around when you open or close something.

Detail

Keyboard people, and anyone whose dock keeps moving under the pointer. It trades flexibility for a memory your hand keeps.

Build note

A fixed grid and a key map. Opens a window or focuses the one that exists. The smallest list of rules in the six.

Main elements

  • 3 by 3 grid
  • Number on each slot
  • Running badge
  • Focused slot lit
  • Empty slot (+)
  • Cmd-1 to Cmd-9

Font note

One digit per slot.

5 Trays: drawn 1:1 at 1024×768 and scaled to fit.

5. Trays

Four labelled drawers, one open at a time

easy

Familiar from

A start menu with the clutter taken out, or folders on a shelf.

What you see

A short bar with the names Make, Play and Tools. Click one and a tray rises with bigger icons and full names, which is the only dock idea here where Unifont labels are always readable. The apps you have open sit at the right as ordinary icons.

Detail

Someone with more than ten apps who would rather read a name than guess an icon.

Build note

A bar, a popover and a list stored in a settings file. You decide which tray an app lives in.

Main elements

  • Tray buttons
  • Open tray with labels
  • Running apps
  • Notch pointing at the button
  • Number keys pick in a tray

Font note

Labels sit under icons, about 8 characters before cutting.

6 Selection-aware dock: drawn 1:1 at 1024×768 and scaled to fit.

6. Selection-aware dock

The right side offers what fits what you have selected

medium

Familiar from

Open With in the right-click menu, moved to where you can reach it.

What you see

The left is the dock as it is today. The right is a dashed zone that changes: select bike.png in Files and it offers Paint, Browser and Send to Mac. Drag the file onto one, or press Enter for the first. Select nothing and the zone is empty.

Detail

Everyone, and it makes the dock do something no other dock does: it knows what you are about to do.

Build note

Needs the file manager to publish its selection and an app-to-type list. Without a type list, the zone shows recent apps. This is the most work of the six, but it needs no new idea about layout.

Main elements

  • Pinned apps (as today)
  • Dashed suggestion zone
  • "for bike.png" label
  • Drop target
  • Enter opens the first

Font note

One short label; icons carry the rest.

Which ones I would build

Most useful: 6 (Selection-aware) and 2 (Recents). Both answer a question the dock never answers: what should I open next? 6 is the one that is new, and the right half can be built on its own while the left stays the dock you have.

Best with Spaces: 1 (Rooms), if Spaces get used a lot. Cheapest: 3 (Edge slots) and 4 (Keypad), a day each, and they cost almost no screen. Least needed: 5 (Trays); it solves a problem only a long app list has.

My pick to try first: 6, then 3 as a switch in Settings.
