1 Side drawer: drawn 1:1 at 1024×768 and scaled to fit.

1. Side drawer

The familiar one: slides in from the right

easy

What feels familiar

Exactly what macOS, Windows and Android users expect: a panel from the right edge, notifications grouped by app, today's events, a Do Not Disturb switch.

What is new

Groups are collapsible, the filter chips (All, Mentions, Today) are built in, and the selected notification can be acted on from the keyboard. Nothing surprising, which is the point.

Who it helps

Everyone. It is the safe default and the baseline to compare the others against.

Cost in a kernel-drawn UI

A glass panel, a list of cards and a group header. The only logic is grouping and a read/unread flag. Easiest of the five.

Main elements

  • Edge drawer (glass)
  • Grouped cards
  • Filter chips
  • Today card
  • Do Not Disturb
  • Clear all / per group

Font note

Card text is cropped around 30 characters in Unifont; a proportional font would fit more.

2 Top shade: drawn 1:1 at 1024×768 and scaled to fit.

2. Top shade

A curtain from the menu bar with quick settings

medium

What feels familiar

Phone-style pull-down: quick toggles and sliders on the left, notifications on the right, one gesture from the menu bar.

What is new

It merges notification centre and control centre into one place, wide enough to use on a desktop. Music controls sit with the toggles, and the shade covers the screen only as far as it needs to.

Who it helps

People who want one panel for everything, and anyone coming from a phone.

Cost in a kernel-drawn UI

Same pieces as the side drawer plus toggles and sliders. The extra cost is a pull-down animation (or just snap it open) and dimming the desktop behind it.

Main elements

  • Menu bar pull-down
  • Quick-setting tiles
  • Sliders
  • Media row
  • Notification list
  • Pull handle

Font note

Tile labels read well in Unifont. Sliders carry the look.

3 Triage inbox: drawn 1:1 at 1024×768 and scaled to fit.

3. Triage inbox

Notifications are a to-do list, not a pile

medium

What feels familiar

A card list in a floating panel, like an email inbox.

What is new

Every notification has three honest actions: Done (gone), Later (comes back when you pick: in an hour, after this task, this evening) and Mute (ask me less). A progress bar shows you are clearing the list, and filter chips follow your Projects.

Who it helps

People who ignore notifications until they pile up, then dismiss them all and lose things.

Cost in a kernel-drawn UI

A timer queue for "Later" (a list of items with a wake-up time), plus per-app mute rules. The panel itself is the same cards with an action row.

Main elements

  • Inbox panel
  • Done / Later / Mute actions
  • Later time picker
  • Progress bar
  • Project filter chips
  • Mute rules

Font note

Action buttons are short words, which suits Unifont fine.

4 Timeline tray: drawn 1:1 at 1024×768 and scaled to fit.

4. Timeline tray

Notifications placed on a time line

medium

What feels familiar

A tray that rises from the dock, like a Quick Look or a share sheet.

What is new

Notifications sit on a line of the last 90 minutes, so you can see when things happened and in what order, with chips for People, Apps, System and what Focus is holding back. Click a card to open where it came from.

Who it helps

People who think in time ("what happened while I was in the build?") and who lose the order of things in a flat list.

Cost in a kernel-drawn UI

A horizontal axis, dots and cards placed by time with a simple collision rule (alternate above and below). Scrubbing back through hours needs scrolling, which is a small amount of extra work.

Main elements

  • Bottom tray
  • Time axis
  • Cards placed by time
  • Type filter chips
  • Held-by-Focus pile
  • Scrub back through the day

Font note

Time labels at 8 px per character are fine. Cards need short titles.

5 Attached to windows: drawn 1:1 at 1024×768 and scaled to fit.

5. Attached to windows

Notifications live where they belong

medium

What feels familiar

Dock badges and a bell, both things people already know.

What is new

Because windows can be tabs, a notification can sit on the exact tab it came from: the build result is a dot on the shell's tab, and the title-bar bell lists only that window's notifications. A global inbox still exists for everything else.

Who it helps

People with lots of windows: what needs you is visible in context, not collected in a drawer you have to open.

Cost in a kernel-drawn UI

Needs each notification to know its source window or tab, plus a small popover. The dots and bell are tiny shapes. It pairs naturally with "Tabs everywhere".

Main elements

  • Dot on the source tab
  • Title-bar bell with count
  • Window-scoped popover
  • Dock badges per app
  • Global inbox link
  • Mute this window

Font note

Badges use one or two digits only, so Unifont is fine.

Design notes

Start with 1 Side drawer. It is the familiar baseline and the cheapest, and everything else can build on it.

Then add the two ideas that change behaviour: 3 Triage (Done, Later, Mute instead of a pile) inside the drawer, and 5 Attached to windows because it makes Tabs everywhere feel complete. 2 Top shade is worth it if you also want a control centre. 4 Timeline is the prettiest and the one I would build last.
