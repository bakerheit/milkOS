A Tabs everywhere: drawn 1:1 at 1024×768 and scaled to fit.

A. Tabs everywhere

Any window can become a tab in any other

medium

What feels familiar

Normal windows with the usual title bar and traffic lights.

What is new

Drag a window onto another one's title bar and it becomes a tab. Files, a terminal and your notes can live in one window. Drag a tab out to split it back.

Who it helps

Anyone with too many windows. It is how people already use browsers, applied to the whole OS.

Cost in a kernel-drawn UI

Needs a small window tree (a window holds a list of children instead of one surface) and drag-and-drop of title bars. The tab strip is plain rounded rects. No new rendering power needed.

Main elements

  • Tab strip in the title bar
  • Drop highlight
  • Drag a window or a tab
  • Merge and split
  • Per-tab close
  • Keyboard: Ctrl-1..9

Font note

Tab titles need to be short at 8 px per letter, so truncate with an ellipsis around 14 characters.

B Projects: drawn 1:1 at 1024×768 and scaled to fit.

B. Projects

Named workspaces that remember everything

medium

What feels familiar

Looks like virtual desktops: a switcher, a shortcut, a menu bar item.

What is new

Each project is a named workspace that remembers which windows were open, where they sat, and which files and notes belong to it. Switch from milkOS to School and your whole setup follows, exactly as you left it.

Who it helps

People who juggle several things (school, work, hobbies) and lose their place every time they switch.

Cost in a kernel-drawn UI

Saving a layout is writing a list of windows (app, file, position, size) to a file, and restoring is reading it back. The switcher is a glass panel with small rectangles for thumbnails, no screenshots needed.

Main elements

  • Project pill in the menu bar
  • Project switcher panel
  • Layout thumbnails
  • Save and restore layouts
  • Per-project files and notes
  • Ctrl-` shortcut

Font note

Unifont is fine. The straw pill in the menu bar tells you where you are.

C Time slider: drawn 1:1 at 1024×768 and scaled to fit.

C. Time slider

Every window can look at its own past

hard

What feels familiar

A normal Files window with one extra clock button in its title bar.

What is new

Click the clock and the window shows its contents as of an earlier time. Deleted files appear again (marked), and you can restore one or copy it out. Nothing is lost by accident.

Who it helps

Everyone who has ever deleted the wrong file. It is Time Machine, but inside the window you are already looking at.

Cost in a kernel-drawn UI

The hard part is underneath: the filesystem has to keep earlier versions (copy-on-write or snapshots). The UI is a slider, a tint over the window and three buttons. Ship it first for Files only, other apps later.

Main elements

  • Clock button in the title bar
  • Past-state tint
  • Scrubber with ticks
  • Deleted-file markers
  • Restore / Copy out / Back to now
  • Snapshot store

Font note

Unifont works. The honey tint and the slider carry the idea.

D Shelf and clipboard: drawn 1:1 at 1024×768 and scaled to fit.

D. Shelf and clipboard

A place to hold things while you move around

easy

What feels familiar

Drag and drop, copy and paste. Nothing you do changes.

What is new

A shelf on the screen edge holds files and snippets while you go and find where they belong, and holding Ctrl-V shows your last few copies to pick from. Both keep things you would normally lose.

Who it helps

Anyone who has copied something, then copied something else, and lost the first thing.

Cost in a kernel-drawn UI

The shelf is a list of references and a drop target. The clipboard history is a ring of the last N items. The glass panels reuse the pre-blurred wallpaper. This is one of the cheapest ideas here.

Main elements

  • Edge shelf (drop target)
  • Held items
  • Clipboard history strip
  • Pick with arrows
  • Clear / pin an item

Font note

Snippets are cropped at about 12 characters. A real font would let you show more.

E Live capsule: drawn 1:1 at 1024×768 and scaled to fit.

E. Live capsule

Background things live in the menu bar

easy

What feels familiar

A normal menu bar and a normal desktop.

What is new

A small capsule in the middle of the menu bar shows what is running right now: a build, a timer, a download, music. Click it to open controls without switching windows, and it disappears when nothing is happening.

Who it helps

Everyone who waits for something while doing something else, which is everyone.

Cost in a kernel-drawn UI

It is one pill and one panel, driven by a short list of background jobs that each report a title, a status and an action. Very little drawing, and it uses glass you already have.

Main elements

  • Capsule in the menu bar
  • Expanding panel
  • Job list (build, timer, download, music)
  • Quick actions
  • Auto-hide when idle

Font note

Unifont works. Keep the capsule text to about 12 characters.

Design notes

These all sit on top of the 1.0 desktop, so you do not have to pick one. A good order to build them: E Live capsule (easiest, instantly useful), D Shelf and clipboard, A Tabs everywhere, B Projects, and C Time slider last because it needs the filesystem to keep old versions.

If I had to pick the pair that would make someone say "oh, nice" the first day: Projects (your whole setup follows you) and Tabs everywhere (the window clutter problem, solved the way browsers already taught people).
